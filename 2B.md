## 1 · TL;DR (30-second read)

An impact note claims a wind-stow scheduling pilot cut **DSM penalty by 31.6%** on a high-wind day, and should be considered for wider use.

The number is **real but the causal claim is not.** Recomputing from `dispatch_blocks.csv`, both figures reproduce **exactly** (penalty **662,444 → 453,086 = −31.6%**; gap **379.0 → 282.8 MWh = −25.4%**). But this is **one conventional day (28 May) vs one AI-assisted day (29 May)** — no control, no repetition, and the "AI" change is just **12 of 96 blocks**. Worse, **28 May is the single highest-penalty day** in the file, and a *conventional* high-wind day (**4 Jun, Rs 403,391**) actually beat the AI day (**Rs 453,086**). So the comparison is against the **worst** day, and the ranking doesn't generalize.

**Defensible verdict → *The 31.6% is arithmetically correct but causally unidentified.*** The strongest claim the evidence supports is the **narrow** one: *on 28–29 May, the AI day settled with a 31.6% lower penalty than the specific day chosen for comparison* — **not** "the pilot reduces DSM penalty by 31.6%." Don't scale on n=1.

## 2 · Scenario in plain English

You are assessing whether the evidence supports an impact note's headline: the wind-stow-aware scheduling pilot **reduced DSM penalty by 31.6%.** You must state the **strongest conclusion you can defend** *and* **what the evidence cannot establish** — and you're told to **recompute what is reproducible from the block data** and **not** to assume a favourable comparison is causal.

Why it matters: a clean-looking percentage from a **single day** is exactly the kind of result that gets a pilot promoted. But a **DSM penalty** is a *banded* deviation charge computed block-by-block, and a one-day-vs-one-day comparison mixes the **method** with the **weather**, the **schedule**, and the **choice of comparison day.** Your job is to separate the arithmetic (which is fine) from the causal inference (which isn't there yet), and to name the measurement that *would* settle it.

## 5 · Verified data findings (the frozen analysis — cite these)

**1. Both headline numbers reproduce exactly.**
From the block data: 28 May submitted **4507.3** MWh / generated **4196.5**; 29 May submitted **4455.2** / generated **4231.1** — matching the note. Daily `dsm_penalty_rs` sums to **662,444 (28 May) → 453,086 (29 May) = −31.6%.** The note's "generation gap" (**379.0 → 282.8**, −25.4%) is the **sum of absolute block deviations** (net schedule−generation is 310.7 → 224.1). Recomputing penalty from the band table independently lands within **0.02%** of the column. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv` + `AI_Pilot_Impact_Note.md`)*

**2. It is n = 1 vs n = 1.**
The comparison is **one** conventional day (28 May) vs **one** AI-assisted day (29 May). `schedule_source = pilot_revision` appears on **only 12 of 96 blocks, all on 29 May** — that single day is the *entire* pilot dataset. No repetition, no control day run in parallel, no same-day counterfactual. The note itself concedes high-wind stow days are **infrequent.** *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**3. The baseline is cherry-picked — a conventional day beat the AI day.**
28 May is the **single highest-penalty day** in the 8-day file (Rs 662,444). But two other high-wind days ran **conventional**: **3 Jun = Rs 497,360** and **4 Jun = Rs 403,391**. The AI day (29 May, **Rs 453,086**) is only **mid-pack — and 4 Jun (conventional) had a *lower* penalty than the AI day.** Comparing the pilot to the worst day inflates the apparent gain. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**4. The penalty is a banded step-function dominated by ~10 stow blocks.**
Deviation is charged by band (>15% → Rs 2.0/kWh). On **both** 28 and 29 May exactly **10 blocks** are in `STOW` and land in the **Rs 2.0** band; those blocks drive most of the rupees. The improvement is the **smaller deviation magnitude** in those blocks after a 12-block `pilot_revision` lowered the submitted schedule ahead of stow — a plausible mechanism, but one concentrated in a handful of weather-dependent blocks on a single day. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv` + `DSM_Commercial_Extract.pdf`)*

**5. The two days are weather-comparable — which helps, but doesn't rescue n=1.**
28 vs 29 May: wind mean **4.8 / 4.8** m/s, gust max **13.1 / 13.4**, cloud **1.0 / 1.0**, availability **98.5% / 98.5%**, curtailment **0 / 0**, firmware **4.7 / 4.7**, **10 / 10** stow blocks. Good matching for a *pair* — but a matched pair is still **one observation**, and it can't estimate effect size or rule out day-specific luck. *(source: `dispatch_blocks.csv`)*

**6. DSM penalty ≠ full commercial impact.**
The extract prices energy at **Rs 2.72/kWh** and notes delivered energy stays valuable "except where separately curtailed." Lowering the *submitted schedule* cuts the deviation penalty but may forgo scheduled value; the honest metric is **net commercial value (energy value − penalty)** across many days — which the note doesn't compute. Penalty-only, one-day framing is a **partial** measure. *(source: `DSM_Commercial_Extract.pdf`)*

## 7 · Traps that lose marks here

> [!CAUTION]
> - **Treating 31.6% as a causal effect.** It's one day vs one day — reproduce it, then refuse the attribution.
> - **Not recomputing.** The rubric asks you to; and doing so reveals the note's "gap" is the *absolute-deviation sum*, not schedule−generation, and that the penalty is band-driven.
> - **Missing the cherry-picked baseline.** 28 May is the worst day; a conventional day (4 Jun) beat the AI day — the single strongest counter-point.
> - **Ignoring the step-function.** Penalty jumps at band edges (>15% → Rs 2.0/kWh) and is dominated by ~10 stow blocks; small deviation changes there swing the rupees.
> - **Judging penalty alone.** Net value = energy (Rs 2.72/kWh) − penalty; a one-metric, one-day view is partial.
> - **Over-swinging to "it's fake."** The number is real and the mechanism is plausible on weather-matched days — the defensible read is *"reproduced but unidentified,"* not *"debunked."*
> - **Forgetting the confound list.** Even matched, 28 vs 29 May differ in date, realized weather, and schedule; only 12 blocks are the actual intervention.

### 8b · Rejected hypotheses + causal limits (→ `## Rejected Hypotheses and Causal Limits`)

- **"The 31.6% is fabricated/wrong"** → **rejected:** it reproduces to the rupee from block data.
- **"The AI method *caused* a 31.6% reduction"** → **not identified:** n=1 vs n=1, no control, no same-day counterfactual; intervention is 12 blocks; a conventional day (4 Jun) beat the AI day.
- **"Lower penalty = better commercial outcome"** → **unproven:** penalty is one part of settlement; net value (energy − penalty) isn't computed.
- **Causally unidentified:** the method's true effect size; its **sign** across typical high-wind days; whether any gain survives a **fair comparator** and **net-value** accounting; how much is method vs realized weather.

### 8c · Next measurement / validation design (→ `## Next Measurement`, pick one)

- **Same-day A/B (strongest):** submit the AI schedule and **shadow-compute** the conventional counterfactual on the *same* day/weather — removes the between-day confound entirely.
- **Matched paired trial:** ≥N AI days vs ≥N conventional days matched on wind/gust/firmware; pre-register the comparator; report a distribution, not one ratio.
- Either way: **hold firmware constant**, and report **net commercial value (energy value − DSM penalty)**, not penalty alone.

### 8d · Recommendation options (→ `## Recommendation`, choose one — all defensible)

| Option | Stance | When to pick it |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **A** *(recommended)* | **Keep piloting; do not publish "31.6%" or expand yet.** Mechanism plausible + days matched, but evidence is n=1 vs the worst day. Decide on scale only after a paired, controlled, net-value trial | You credit the mechanism but demand identification |
| **B** | **Continue evaluation, reframe the metric.** Replace penalty-only with net value and a pre-specified control before any claim | You want to fix the measurement first |
| **C** | **Insufficient evidence — pause claims entirely** until a same-day counterfactual or matched trial exists | You want maximum caution |

### 8e · The narrower claim the evidence *does* support (put this in `## Judgment`)

> *On 28–29 May, the AI-assisted day settled with a 31.6% lower DSM penalty than the specific conventional day chosen for comparison — reproducible from the block data — plausibly because a 12-block schedule revision reduced deviation in the wind-stow blocks. This is a single weather-matched pair, not evidence that the pilot reduces DSM penalty by 31.6% in general.*
